# Why?

## v1

I've been thinking about *As Slow As Possible* a bunch recently because I've been making a build for Gros Joueurs. Because of that I found myself thinking (I think in a stairwell??) about how it's not really as slow as *possible*? It's just really slow. So the point of making a sequel was to explore this question of what as slow as possible might be (and particularly I suppose what it might *feel* like). And actually maybe the key is around *player-enforced* slowness, as opposed from it being something you suffer through/relish from the game. It's how slow can you bear to go, when you could go fast. That kind of idea.

So is there a why in there? If I were to pitch something more research adjacent I'd say it's to do with

- The experience of slow time and agency?
- Questions about the meaning of qualitative ideas like slowness in a discrete system like computers and numbers? (The idea of a smallest possible movement – like a videogame Planck length – meaning there is a "true slowest"?)
- The effort/magic/futility of trying to get a player to care about slowness (though leaderboards? "I bet you can't"isms?)

Not the clearest, but that's the gist.
